Jobsmith

Your AI career agent, running on your own machine.

Jobsmith runs your job search the way a good talent agent would run it. It learns your career, builds your case, finds where the openings in your field actually get posted, tailors every application, and gets you ready for the interview.

You make every call. It drafts, you approve, you send. Free, open source, and yours to keep.

Before you start, the one honest catch: Jobsmith runs inside Claude Code, which needs a paid Claude plan (Pro or above). Everything else is free and optional. Better to know that now than five steps in.

Your search becomes a pipeline, not a pile of tabs

Every opportunity is one row, and its status says whose move it is. Nothing sits in limbo, nothing gets forgotten.

Sourced› Approved› Tailored› Applied› Interviewing› Offer› Closed

Highlighted means the ball is in your court.

You move rows yourself, by dragging a card or editing a cell. Jobsmith reads your tracker at the start of every session and picks up from there, so you never have to tell an AI what you already told your own spreadsheet. Approved rows are work orders: next time you talk, it builds those applications.

What it does

Six modules, each usable on its own. Start anywhere. If all you want is a job sourcer, use the sourcer.

Reads your resume, honestly

It plays your career back to you, including what your resume undersells. Most experienced people undersell, and the gap between what you did and what your resume claims is where interviews get lost.

Built

Builds your brag doc

A structured interview that turns your experience into defensible accomplishments. Every number gets questioned. Estimates stay marked as estimates instead of hardening into false precision.

Built

Finds your industry's channels

Not tech boards. It researches where roles in your specific field get posted, association boards included, verifies each one is real, and writes you the exact job-alert queries to set up.

Built

Sweeps for roles, on a schedule

Scores each find against your profile and filters the weak ones out instead of padding the list. Ask for daily or weekly and the pipeline stays fed without you having to remember.

Built

Tailors each application

A resume and cover letter per role, built from your verified material. It names the gaps between you and the posting out loud, and it writes the letter in your voice rather than in the voice of every other AI cover letter.

Built

Preps you, then helps you negotiate

Company research, likely questions mapped to your real stories, mock interviews that drill the hard parts, and offer analysis when you get there.

The rules it will not break

These are written into the agent itself, not just onto this page. You can read them in the repo.

  • It never applies or sends anything for you.Mass auto-apply tools damage your standing with recruiters. Jobsmith drafts; you review and send. Fewer, better applications is the entire strategy.
  • It never invents a claim about you.Every line traces back to your own materials or your own words. Ask where any sentence came from and it will show you. If something could not be defended in an interview, it does not go in your resume.
  • It never guesses a link.Every URL it hands you was checked live in that session. Lookalike company domains are common enough that a guess is not just sloppy, it is dangerous.
  • Your data never leaves your machine.Everything about you lives in one local folder. No server, no account, no analytics. Delete the folder and Jobsmith knows nothing about you.
  • It will not nag you.A stalled pipeline gets a plain observation and an offer to help. Job searching is hard enough without a robot expressing disappointment in you.

Written to be read

Jobsmith is plain markdown, not a black box. Its judgment is legible, and you can change any of it. Two real excerpts:

From the sourcing skill. A location filter must never hide remote-eligible roles. This is not optional; it is the single most common way a sweep silently loses the best role in the market.

From the brag doc interview. Sourced and confident: record as fact. Recalled and plausible: record with a mark and the word estimate. Unverifiable: keep the claim qualitative rather than inventing precision.

If you want the design behind it, the architecture doc covers how the pieces compose and why.

Get started

Two paths to the same place. Pick the one that sounds like you.

You need Claude Code and a paid Claude plan. Then:

git clone https://github.com/joseBenavides/jobsmith.git
cd jobsmith
# drop your resume (any format) into my/intake/
claude

Say hello. Jobsmith notices it has not met you, checks which tools it has, and offers to start. Onboarding is long but stops and resumes cleanly, so you can do it in pieces.

Worth connecting first: the Notion connector, so it builds your pipeline tracker as a real database with filtered views instead of a spreadsheet. A mail connector is useful later, for triaging job alerts into the pipeline. Both optional, both an improvement.

  1. Get a paid Claude plan

    Jobsmith runs inside a tool called Claude Code, and Claude Code requires a paid plan (Pro or above). The free tier will not work. Start at claude.ai.

  2. Install Claude Code

    Follow Anthropic's install guide. It runs in your computer's terminal, the plain text window where you type commands. That sounds intimidating and turns out to be about four words of typing.

  3. Download Jobsmith

    Go to the GitHub page, click the green Code button, and choose Download ZIP. No account needed. Unzip it somewhere you will find again, like your Documents folder. You now have a folder called jobsmith.

  4. Put your resume in it

    Open the jobsmith folder, then my, then intake. Drop your resume in there. Any format, any condition, and out of date is fine. If you have more than one version, add them all; the differences are genuinely useful.

  5. Open a terminal in that folder

    This is the only fiddly step, and you only do it once.

    Windows: right-click the jobsmith folder and choose Open in Terminal. If you do not see that option, open the folder first, then right-click the empty space inside it.

    Mac: right-click the jobsmith folder, go to Services, and choose New Terminal at Folder. If that option is missing, open the Terminal app, type cd followed by a space, then drag the folder onto the window and press Enter.

  6. Type two things

    Type claude and press Enter. When it is ready, type hello and press Enter.

    That is the whole install. Jobsmith will introduce itself, find your resume, and take it from there. It asks a lot of questions early, because that is what makes everything after it good. Stop whenever you like and pick up later; nothing is lost.

  7. Optional, and worth it

    Notion gives you a real pipeline table with filters and a drag-and-drop board, and Jobsmith builds it for you. The free plan is plenty. See Notion's setup guide, or just ask Jobsmith to walk you through it.

    A mail connector lets it read job-alert emails and file the good ones into your pipeline. A tip either way: set up a separate free email address for your search. It keeps your real inbox clean and your search discreet.
