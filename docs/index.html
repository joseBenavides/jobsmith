Jobsmith

An AI career agent you run yourself.

Jobsmith is a set of instructions that turn an AI coding assistant into a career agent. It reads your resume, interviews you properly about the work you have done, researches where your field actually posts its jobs, and writes an application for each role you decide to pursue.

It runs on your own computer, out of a folder you can open and read. It drafts; you send.

Get started Source on GitHub

One thing to know before you invest any time. Jobsmith runs inside Claude Code, which needs a paid Claude plan. Everything else about it is free. It is worth saying that here rather than four steps into an install.

The first conversation is the long one

You hand it whatever resume you already have. It reads the whole thing and tells you what it sees: the shape of your career, the strongest things on the page, and the places you have undersold yourself.

That last part is usually where the interesting material is. Experienced people undersell in predictable ways. A rollout you led gets written up as one you participated in. The number of people who report to you appears nowhere on the page. A decade of training new hires shows up as a single line near the bottom.

You correct whatever it got wrong, and that correction matters more than anything it got right, because everything after this is built from what it now knows about you.

Then it asks what you are looking for, and goes and researches where those jobs are actually posted in your field. For most people that turns out not to be the big job boards. It is an association board, one or two specialist recruiters, and a short list of employers worth watching directly. It opens each one to check it is real and still carries work at your level before it writes anything down. Last, it sets up somewhere to keep track: a Notion database it builds for you, or a spreadsheet if you would rather.

Set aside an hour or two, and know that you can stop partway and pick it up another day without losing anything.

After that, sessions are short

Your search lives as a list of roles, each one at a stage.

Sourced› Approved› Tailored› Applied› Interviewing› Offer› Closed

The marked stages are the ones waiting on you.

You move things along by editing the list yourself, dragging a card or changing a cell, the same way you would with any tracker. Jobsmith reads it at the start of every session, so you are never in the position of telling an AI something you already told your own spreadsheet.

Anything you have marked as approved is work it can pick up. It checks the posting is still live, works out what the role is really asking for, and writes you a resume and a cover letter for it. Then it tells you which of the requirements you cannot actually evidence, which is the part most tools skip.

What ends up on your disk

All of it in one folder, in plain text you can read without Jobsmith running.

my/
  profile.md          what you are looking for, and what you will not take
  brag-doc.md         your accomplishments, with sources noted
  channels.md         where your field posts jobs, and the alerts to set
  pipeline.csv        every role and where it stands
  kits/
    acme-director/    one folder per application
      resume.md
      cover-letter.md
      decode.md       what the job wants, and where you fall short
      log.md          who you spoke to and what happened

Nothing is transmitted anywhere except to the model you chose to run it with. There is no account and no server. If you delete the folder, Jobsmith knows nothing about you.

What it will not do

It will not apply to anything for you, or send an email as you. Everything it produces is a draft that sits there until you read it and send it yourself. Tools that fire off hundreds of applications tend to work against the person using them, and the whole design here assumes you would rather send eight good applications than eighty forgettable ones.

It will not make anything up about you. Every line in a resume it writes traces back to something in your own materials or something you told it, and you can ask where any sentence came from. When you give it a number it cannot source, it records the number as your estimate and keeps it that way rather than quietly promoting it to a fact. This is less about principle than about the interview, where a claim that cannot survive a follow-up question is worse than no claim at all.

It also will not nag you. A search that has gone quiet gets a plain observation and an offer to help, which is the appropriate amount of pressure from software.

It is readable, and you can change it

Jobsmith is markdown files, not a compiled product. Its judgment is written down in ordinary sentences, which means you can read what it will do before you run it and edit anything you disagree with. Two lines from the instructions it follows:

A location filter must never hide remote-eligible roles. This is not optional; it is the single most common way a sweep silently loses the best role in the market.

Sourced and confident: record as fact. Recalled and plausible: record with a mark and the word estimate. Unverifiable: keep the claim qualitative rather than inventing precision.

If you want the reasoning behind how it is put together, that is written up in the architecture notes.

Where it currently stands

Onboarding, the brag doc interview, channel research, the tracker, sourcing sweeps, and tailored applications are built and working. Interview preparation, mock interviews, and offer negotiation are written but not built yet.

It is early, and it has been used by a handful of people rather than a thousand. If something breaks or reads badly, an issue on GitHub is genuinely useful.

Getting it running

You need Claude Code and a paid Claude plan.

git clone https://github.com/joseBenavides/jobsmith.git
cd jobsmith
# put your resume, any format, into my/intake/
claude

Then say hello. It works out that it has not met you before and offers to start.

Worth connecting the Notion connector first if you use Notion, since it will build you a proper database with filtered views instead of a spreadsheet. A mail connector is useful later for reading job alerts into the pipeline. Neither is required.

  1. Get a paid Claude plan

    Jobsmith runs inside Claude Code, and Claude Code needs a paid plan. The free tier will not run it. Start at claude.ai.

  2. Install Claude Code

    Anthropic's guide walks through it. It runs in your terminal, which is the plain window where you type commands rather than click things. It is less alarming than it sounds; you will type about four words into it.

  3. Download Jobsmith

    On the GitHub page, click the green Code button and choose Download ZIP. You do not need an account. Unzip it somewhere you will find again, such as your Documents folder.

    The folder will be called jobsmith-main. That is normal. Rename it to jobsmith if you like; the steps below work either way.

  4. Put your resume inside it

    Open the folder, then my, then intake, and drop your resume in. Any format is fine and so is an old one. If you have more than one version, put them all in, because the differences between them are useful.

  5. Open a terminal in that folder

    This is the only awkward step, and you do it once.

    Windows: right-click the folder and choose Open in Terminal. If that is not in the menu, open the folder first and right-click the empty space inside it.

    Mac: right-click the folder, go to Services, then New Terminal at Folder. If that is missing, open the Terminal app, type cd and a space, then drag the folder onto the window and press Enter.

  6. Type two words

    Type claude and press Enter. When it is ready, say hello.

    That is the install. It will introduce itself, find your resume, and take it from there. Stop whenever you need to; it remembers where you were.

  7. Optional extras

    If you use Notion, connecting it means Jobsmith builds you a real database to track everything, with a board you can drag cards around on. The free plan covers it. Notion's guide explains the setup, or you can ask Jobsmith to walk you through it.

    Connecting email lets it read job alerts and file the promising ones for you. Either way, a separate free email address for your search is worth setting up. It keeps your normal inbox clean, and it keeps the search to yourself.
